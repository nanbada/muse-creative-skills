# Style prompt blocks

Append one block (verbatim core, then subject-specific words) to the
subject description. All blocks are English; keep the technique words —
they are what makes the output read as watercolor rather than digital art.

## 1. Floral-fashion fantasy

`delicate watercolor fashion illustration, fine line art, translucent
flowing dress made of blooming flowers and ethereal fabric, soft pastel
color palette, wet-on-wet watercolor blooming, subtle pigment splatters,
cold-press watercolor paper texture, clean high-key background, magical
stardust details`

Notes: translucent layered washes; soft wet-on-wet gradients; pastel and
luminous tones; airy light background so the bleeding shines.

## 1b. Floral-fashion, vivid reference (attached refs)

Use when the user wants the vivid attached-reference look instead of the
soft pastel default. Person first, flowers second — the concept is
watercolor flowers, but the figure carries the picture.

`slender elegant fashion illustration, figure reading clearly as a
person, person first, profile or downcast three-quarter pose, small
delicate face, soft lightly sketched, eyes nearly closed, minimal
features, upper body either a fitted
hourglass bodice with a defined waist and a gentle bust curve or
lushly covered with abundant flowers, hourglass line whenever the upper
body shows, elegant and never exaggerated, dress mostly translucent
wash with a few distinct small petals as vivid accents, petal material
suggested by wash color and a few small petals, never a mosaic of large
petals, flowers much smaller than the head, never covering the face,
legs and shoes readable unless a long floral dress skirt covers them by
design, flower crown of small
blossoms, vivid yet restrained flower colors on a pale
cream or pale pink luminous ground with generous empty negative space,
fine delicate ink lines, thin swirling calligraphic flourishes used
sparingly, small round pigment droplets as beads, never running drips
or downward streaks, soft watercolor blooming kept clean and
controlled, transparent layered petals with crisp clean edges, elegant
quiet grace, East Asian restraint: negative space is composition, never
crowd the frame, wet-on-wet transparency`

Notes (precise re-analysis of the 5 attached refs, 2026-11-05):
- Hierarchy: the figure always reads as a person — face always clear.
  Tiered coverage rule (2026-11-05 user correction, this supersedes the
  absolute "never cover legs" clause): face is never covered, hard rule.
  Legs and shoes MAY be covered when flowers constitute the dress style
  itself — a long floral gown or a cascading bouquet skirt falling over
  the legs. That coverage is the garment, not an obstruction, and is not
  a failure. The upper torso follows the hourglass-conditional rule
  below, not a never-cover rule. What still fails: face covered, or
  flowers piled so densely that the posture and silhouette stop reading
  as a person. The skirt's translucent wash may spread wide, but the
  blooms inside it stay accents, except where the dress itself is made
  of them.
- Scale (this supersedes the earlier "blooms larger than the torso,
  exploding beyond the silhouette" wording): the largest single bloom
  is at most about head size — the yellow hip bloom in ref 42 is the
  ceiling, and even it covers no body. Ref 41 (green): skirt mostly
  translucent wash with small scattered blooms, legs and heels fully
  visible. Ref 43 (pastel): shortest skirt, longest visible legs, the
  most person-first of the set. Ref 44 (pastel large): flowers cascade
  as a gown but stay translucent with face and torso as the focal point.
  Ref 45 (navy): small scattered blooms and sparkles on translucent dark
  layers, full body line readable.
- Transparency and elegance: layered translucent petals, luminous
  color, body line showing through or beside the wash — never an opaque
  pile of flowers hiding the figure. Vivid but restrained, emotional and
  detailed, never flat saturation or wall-to-wall density.
- Petal-material dress (standing, 2026-11-05 user rule): the dress is
  made of petal material, not a garment with petals stuck on it. Some
  areas show distinct petals; areas that read as clothing are rendered
  in petal colors as watercolor washes. Petals visibly glued or pasted
  onto a plain dress fail; so does a plain dress with a few flowers
  sprinkled on top. Wash-dominant (2026-11-06 v4 correction): the
  petal material reads through translucent wash color plus a few small
  distinct petals; a mosaic of large distinct petals fails even when
  the material idea is right.
- Elegance gap — v4 (2026-11-06, user rejected v3: quality differs
  greatly from the ref; the ref's elegant artistic feeling is not
  reflected): (1) petal scale — distinct petals/blooms much smaller
  than the head, about one-third at most; a petal bigger than the head
  makes the dress a heavy mosaic. (2) density/saturation — pale wash
  dominates; vivid primaries appear only as a few small accents, never
  dense max-saturation fill. (3) negative space — background mostly
  empty paper; maple leaves at most 2–3, droplet splashes minimal.
  (4) face — soft lightly sketched, eyes nearly closed; never a
  realistic profile with eye detail. (5) dress texture — bleeding
  wash, fine pen lines, droplet dots; never hard shapes with clear
  borders. Reconciliation with the color-mood rule: "vivid over pastel"
  under a bright sky means vivid ACCENTS, not vivid fill.
- East Asian inflection (borrowed from the pencil-line-poetry manner,
  line and space only): fine delicate lines, generous negative space,
  quiet grace, one calm emotion, never a crowded frame. Do NOT import
  that skill's tiny-character / giant-nature scale play — it contradicts
  person-first and is excluded here.
- Hourglass upper body, conditional (standing, 2026-11-05 user rule,
  clarified the same day): the hourglass applies WHEN the upper body is
  visible — a defined narrow waist, a gentle bust curve above it, the
  hip curve flowing below into the skirt. Elegant fashion-illustration
  hourglass, never exaggerated. When the upper body shows, the waist
  line must read even under the translucent wash; a visible straight or
  boxy torso fails. But there is no obligation to show the upper body or
  waist at all: the upper-body area may instead be lushly covered with
  abundant flowers. Showing the torso is a choice; hourglass is the form
  it takes when shown.
- Color follows mood (standing, 2026-11-05 user rule): flower colors
  match the scene atmosphere, never pastel by default. A bright blue
  autumn sky with sunlight calls for vivid primary colors; soft, dreamy,
  or dusk moods keep pastels. Match saturation to the light, and let the
  background set it — blue sky and sun mean vivid, not washed-out.
  Transparency always wins (2026-11-05 user correction): vivid means
  vivid transparent washes, never opaque saturated blocks or dense
  paint. If vividness and transparency conflict, keep the natural
  transparent watercolor feel of the references — light airy washes,
  color glowing through transparency, delicate lines, breathing space.
  A dense opaque flower mass fails even when its colors match the mood.
  Reconciliation (2026-11-06 v4 correction): vivid means vivid accents,
  not dominant fill — v3 read "vivid primaries" as dense fill and
  failed on elegance. The pale wash stays dominant.
- Heels (when visible) and crown always match the dress flowers. A muted
  dress-shape with a detailed front face fails, and so does any flower
  mass that hides the face or the whole posture (upper torso lushly
  flower-covered, and legs and shoes under a long floral dress,
  excepted).
Clean-transparency rule (standing, 2026-11-05 user correction — this
supersedes the earlier "splatters and drips / bleeding beyond lines"
wording in this block): vivid but emotional and detailed; flowers
transparent and clean; even soft or blurred passages stay clean, never
runny, smeared, or muddy; paint appears only as round droplet beads,
never as paint flowing downward; splatter only as discrete dots.
Running drips, downward streaks, and muddy blurred washes fail this
block.

## 2. European storybook atmosphere

`classic storybook watercolor illustration, detailed ink and wash
linework, warm atmospheric lighting, cozy European town aesthetic,
textured rough watercolor paper, rich pigments, nostalgic and whimsical
mood`

Notes: warm glowing lantern lights; wet road reflections; muted European
vintage tones; deep indigo night skies; visible rough paper grain.

## 3. Botanical, loose, and ink-and-wash

- Loose wash: `spontaneous loose watercolor style, expressive
  brushstrokes, dynamic water blooms and color bleeds, organic fluid
  textures`
- Botanical: `precise botanical watercolor, fine detail, layered
  transparent washes, natural specimen accuracy, white paper background`
- Ink-and-wash: `pen and ink watercolor, expressive hatching, monochrome
  ink line with selective color washes, textured paper`

## Aspect ratio defaults

| Subject | Ratio |
|---|---|
| Full-body fashion / portrait | 3:4 or 9:16 |
| Panoramic landscape / street scene | 16:9 or 4:3 |
| Standard artistic canvas | 1:1 |
