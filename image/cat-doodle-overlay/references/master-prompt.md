# Master Prompt — Cat Doodle Overlay

Fill the [BRACKETS], then paste the whole block as the image prompt,
with the user's photo attached.

```
Transform the attached photo into a mixed-media editorial illustration:
PHOTOGRAPHY x WHITE HAND-DRAWN CAT DOODLE.

## 1. Preserve the photo
Keep the uploaded photo's real content as complete as possible: real
scene, subject buildings / landmarks / people / roads / water /
mountains / trees / sky, original composition, camera angle, perspective,
light direction, atmosphere, photographic texture. Do not change the
location, redesign the subject architecture, or alter the core real
spatial relationships. The photo's key subjects must stay clear, real,
and recognizable.

## 2. The cat
Draw ONE oversized white hand-drawn line-art cat ON TOP of the photo.
The cat must interact with the real environment in a way that is clever,
funny, natural, and instantly readable — as if it were really there:
playing, napping, peeking, pouncing, leaning on something, stretching a
paw, nuzzling, chasing, hiding, or stretching out.

Interaction: [INTERACTION — one concrete action tied to THIS photo,
e.g. "the cat hugs the tower with both front paws as if it were a
scratching post, tail curling around the antenna"]

Scale: surreal scale play — the cat is clearly larger than life next to
the scene, but at about 70% of a full giant-monster scale: slightly
restrained, never overwhelming the frame. Its pose and perspective must
feel physically plausible inside the photo's space.

Cat style: simple, natural, healing, slightly childlike white line art —
white marker / white chalk / fine white pen drawn directly on the photo.
Line weight varies slightly. Outlines clear but never over-complex. NO
realistic fur detail, NO refined manga style, NO heavy outlines, NO 3D
cartoon feel, NO sticker look.

Cat face: minimal features — gentle, curious, dopey, a little
mischievous, healing, humorous. It must feel present and involved, not
decorative.

## 3. Accents
The doodle layer is white line art FIRST. You may add a small amount of
ONE low-saturation yellow (mustard / warm / golden) for tiny focal
points only: small stars, small hearts, small flowers, small sparkles, a
tiny butterfly or fish, the small prop the cat is playing with, or a warm
highlight cue. Keep yellow restrained — never let it dominate.

Sparingly, you may also add: motion lines, a tiny heart, a tiny star, a
wagging-tail line, paw action lines.

Optional caption: ONE short handwritten phrase in the SAME white as the
cat (e.g. "caught you!", "mine now", "shhh..."). Tiny and casual; it
must never dominate. [CAPTION, or omit entirely]

## 4. Composition
The photograph remains the visual foundation. Place the cat using the
photo's own structure — open sky, lawn, wall, water, road, building
edges, horizon, windows, trees, bridges. Never cover the most important
architectural details. The cat and the real objects must share accurate
spatial, perspective, and action relationships: readable at first
glance, delightful at second glance.

## 5. Mood
Minimal, clean, humorous, designed, narrative, with visual contrast and
a healing feel — high-end creative-ad / editorial-illustration quality.
The final effect: reality quietly invaded by an imaginative cat.
Photography carries the real; lines carry the imagination.

## Negative
Do not alter the core structure of the real architecture. Do not redo
the background. Do not add extra characters. Do not draw many cats. Do
not fill the frame with doodles. No generic sticker effect. No 3D
cartoon. No black heavy outlines. No complex manga style. No realistic
colored cat. No large solid color fills. No titles, no logos, no
watermarks. Keep the photo's subject clearly recognizable, keep the
photographic texture, and keep the doodle naturally fused with the
photo. Clever, not merely cute.
```
